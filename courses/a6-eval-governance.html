Part 1 · the course's synthesis - a1-a5 as eight demands

The scorecard to demand before any model ships 15 min live

One page, eight slots. Each slot is a session of this course compressed into a demand, and each has a failure smell you can detect from the leadership seat: a metric with no base rate, a price with no owner, a judge score with no agreement number. A team with real eval discipline fills this in an afternoon. A team that cannot fill it is telling you something more important than any metric would.

The confusion matrix with the base rate. Never a bare accuracy - a1's whole lesson. "96.8% accurate" on a 3.2% base rate can describe a model that finds nobody. The matrix plus the base rate is the minimum honest unit of reporting.

The cost matrix, with named owners for the prices. What does a false positive cost, what does a false negative cost, and WHO signed off on those numbers? A price nobody owns is a guess wearing a suit. This is the slot most often missing in real reviews.

The chosen operating point, and who chose it. A threshold is a business decision, not a default. If the answer is "0.5, because that is the default," nobody made the decision - it made itself.

For LLM systems: quality metrics WITH their grader's human-agreement number. A judge score without agreement against human labels is a rumor. The bar from a3: "over 80%" agreement, which equals human-human agreement - a judge cannot beat the humans it imitates.

Latency (p95 TTFT) and cost per query, against declared budgets. Speed and price are success criteria on Anthropic's own list, not infra footnotes. "Fast enough" is not a budget; "p95 TTFT under 1.5s at under $0.04 per query" is.

The golden set size, and when it last ran. A dated artifact, not an assurance. 20-50 real cases minimum, and a run date measured in days, not quarters.

The regression-eval status - still ~100%? Regression suites guard what already works and are meant to hold at ~100%; any dip blocks the change. A green regression line is the cheapest sleep aid in AI.

The online metric each offline number is a proxy for. Every offline score must name the online metric it predicts, which in turn serves a business goal. If the chain cannot be named, slot 8 is empty - and so is the claim.

LiveHow to use it without becoming the bottleneck4 min▶

The scorecard is a demand, not a form you police. Three usage rules keep it light:

  • The team fills it, you read it. Fifteen minutes of reading replaces an hour of interrogation, because the blanks do the interrogating.
  • Blanks are findings, not failures. "We have no cost matrix owner" said out loud is progress - it names the exact next meeting. What you are eliminating is the silent blank.
  • One page, enforced. A 30-page eval appendix is how weak numbers hide. The one-page constraint forces the eight numbers that matter to stand in the open.
The fastest read Check slots 2 and 8 first - the cost matrix owners and the proxy chain. In practice they are the most often missing, and a team that has both almost always has the other six.

Part 2 · covers Zheng et al. judge bar, Rules of ML #39

Vendor grilling - discipline vs demo theater 14 min live

Every vendor deck shows a benchmark win and a delighted-user quote. The same four questions cut through all of them, because they ask for the numbers demo theater cannot fake. None require you to know the math - only to know that the numbers must exist.

LiveThe four questions, and the answers to expect8 min▶
  • "Show me the retention data, not the engagement data." Engagement is the proxy; retention is closer to the money. A vendor who pivots back to engagement three times is telling you which number they have.
  • "What is your judge's agreement with human labels?" If their quality scores come from an LLM judge, this number must exist. The bar: "over 80%", which equals human-human agreement. "Our judge is GPT-4-class" is not an answer - it is the setup for the question.
  • "What happened to your metrics when the base model last changed?" Real discipline produces a before-and-after regression report within a day of any model change. If they cannot show one, they are not measuring - they are hoping.
  • "Walk me through one case your system failed last month." Teams with golden sets and production sampling have failure cases catalogued and love talking about them. Demo theater has never seen a failure, which tells you nobody looked.
Real world

Concepts outlive vendor products. OpenAI's own evals platform is being deprecated on Nov 30, 2026. Any process built on a vendor's eval buttons inherits the vendor's roadmap. The concepts - golden sets, graders, agreement bars, regression suites - transfer intact across every platform. Demand the concepts, not the product tour.

LiveThe closing frame: the two-hop proxy chain6 min▶

Rule #39's insight, and the frame to hold every eval claim in: eval score -> online metric -> business goal. Two hops, and each hop can break. The offline eval predicts the online metric imperfectly (hop 1); the online metric serves the business goal imperfectly (hop 2). A benchmark win is a hop-1 event - real, worth having, and two broken links away from revenue.

The discipline is not cynicism - it is precision: celebrate hop 1 as hop 1. "The eval improved, which historically predicts a lift in conversion, which we will confirm in the next release" is a leader's sentence. "The eval improved, ship it and book the value" is how killer 2 gets funded for another quarter.

Self-studyPut the grilling in writing3 min read▶

Questions asked in a meeting can be charmed away; questions in a procurement document cannot. Three clauses worth adding to your next RFP or renewal:

  • Evidence clause: "Vendor will provide, for the proposed system, its evaluation methodology including golden set size, grading method, and - where an LLM judge is used - the judge's measured agreement with human labels."
  • Change clause: "Vendor will notify us before changes to the underlying model version, and will provide before-and-after regression results for any such change."
  • Exit clause: "Evaluation artifacts (golden sets, scoring rubrics, historical results) relating to our deployment are deliverables, portable at contract end."

The third clause is the quiet power move: it makes the concepts yours even when the product is not - the same reason this course teaches taxonomies, not vendor buttons.

Part 3 · the standing routine

The quarterly eval review 10 min live

A scorecard checked once is a photograph; a5 taught you why photographs fail. The fix is a review cadence run like a financial audit - scheduled, owned, and boring in the best way. Quarterly is the default; monthly if the domain moves fast.

LiveWhat the quarterly review checks, and who owns what7 min▶

Three checks, thirty minutes, four times a year:

  • Golden set freshness. Do the 20-50 cases still look like this quarter's real traffic? Users invent new phrasings; the set must follow them.
  • Drift check. Sampled production scores vs the launch baseline - is the gap growing? This is a5's killer 3 on a leash.
  • Price re-validation. Are the FP and FN costs still true? Margins move, order values move - a2's operating point is only as good as the prices behind it.

And the ownership map, so the review never becomes everyone's job and therefore no one's:

AssetOwnerAccountable for
The prices (cost matrix)The product ownerFP/FN costs current, business KPI named
The metricsThe DS teamRight metric per decision, base rates attached
The CI gateThe platform teamNothing ships unmeasured, regression suite ~100%

Notice the pattern: it is the skew ladder's three owners again, now with standing responsibilities instead of incident response. Governance is just incident response moved before the incident.

The 30-minute agenda, verbatim Minute 0-10: golden set freshness (owner: DS, with the product owner sampling five cases). Minute 10-20: drift check against the launch baseline (platform brings the chart). Minute 20-30: price re-validation (product owner brings this quarter's FP/FN costs). If any check fails, the scorecard reopens - not the codebase.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Which scorecard slot is most often missing in real reviews?

Computing metrics is comfortable, technical work; owning the FP/FN prices means signing your name to business assumptions. That is why slot 2 goes blank - and why a priced sheet with an owner is the strongest single signal of eval discipline.

2 · A team reports a big offline eval win. What is the proxy-chain rule?

Rule #39: launch metrics are proxies for long-term goals. The win is real AND two hops from money - so name the online metric it should move, confirm it in the next release, and never skip a hop in the story you tell upward.

3 · A vendor says "our LLM judge scores us 4.6 out of 5." When does that number mean anything?

A judge score is a model output, not a measurement, until it is calibrated against human labels. The bar is "over 80%" agreement - which equals human-human agreement, the ceiling a judge cannot beat. No agreement number, no meaning.
